Web Website design Luxury real estate 2026

Casey Margenau Fine Homes & Estates

A homepage that reads as luxury from the first frame — for the Northern Virginia team with more than $4B in career sales.

caseymargenau.com
The brief

Make the site feel like the homes

Casey Margenau's team sells the most considered houses in McLean, Great Falls, Vienna and Oakton. The site that represented them did not carry that weight. It had to speak to buyers and sellers at once, surface the numbers that build trust, and never look like a template.

Client
Casey Margenau Fine Homes & Estates
Industry
Luxury real estate
Services
Web design, UX/UI, front-end
Deliverables
Homepage, three layout options
Year
2026
01 — Hero

A photograph, a scrim, three numbers

The page opens on a stone estate at dusk under a deep navy wash, with a light-weight Inter headline doing the talking. Under it, the three numbers that matter to a luxury client — career volume, homes sold, years — and two calls to action: call, or ask.

  • Parallax photograph with a navy scrim
  • Inter at 200 with a 500 emphasis
  • Red for the one thing to do next
02 — Two paths

Sellers on navy, buyers on white

Every visitor is one or the other. The split panel lets each find their side in a glance — a four-point promise, then one link — with an oversized “SELL” and “BUY” ghosted into the ground so the choice reads from across the room.

  • Two audiences, no tabs
  • Numbered promises, hairline-ruled
  • One link each, underlined in the accent
03 — Featured property

One listing, framed like a print

A single property gets the room a gallery would give it: a tall photograph, a red “Featured” badge, a navy bar carrying price and address, and a pair of buttons — view all listings, or schedule a private tour.

  • Swappable as listings change
  • Price and address in one bar
  • Built to grow into a listings page
04 — How we work

Four steps on navy

Listing preparation, luxury marketing, pricing and negotiation, contract to closing. Set as four cards on the deep navy with oversized step numbers, so the process reads as a promise rather than a checklist.

  • Numbered 01–04 in the slate blue
  • Cards ruled in a hairline of white
  • Sits between credentials and testimonials
05 — Insight

The latest article, and the questions before it

Three FAQs on a cream ground answer what a first visit asks — how to choose an agent, what marketing to expect, why local experience matters — and the newest blog post follows with a full-height photograph, so the page ends on expertise before it asks for the call.

  • FAQ accordion in a single white card
  • One article, image-led
  • A closing band with address and phone
Imagery

Photography direction

Homes photographed at the hour they look best — a stone estate under a summer sky, a modern villa at noon, a timber house at dusk — and held under the navy wherever text has to sit on them.
